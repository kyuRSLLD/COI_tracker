What to Check on a Subcontractor's ACORD 25 Before They Step on a Job Site

November 18, 20267 min read

A sub hands you an ACORD 25 certificate of insurance. The company name looks right, the limits say $1M, and the ADDL INSD box is checked. You file it and put the crew on the schedule.

Two months later, water is coming through a ceiling. Your attorney reads the same page: the named insured is the owner’s personal name, the policy lapsed before work started, and the endorsement you assumed existed was never issued.

The certificate is designed to look reassuring. Its own text says it is issued as a matter of information only, confers no rights, and does not amend coverage. Read it the way an auditor would.

Five zones on the form

  1. Producer — the broker who issued it.
  2. Insured — the entity being certified.
  3. Insurers A–F — the carriers and their NAIC numbers.
  4. Coverage grid — GL, auto, umbrella, WC, plus the ADDL INSD and SUBR WVD columns.
  5. Description, certificate holder, cancellation — project wording, your name, notice terms.

Most mistakes happen in the first and last zones.

The nine checks

1. Named insured matches the legal entity on the subcontract. If you contracted with Apex Contracting LLC, the certificate must read Apex Contracting LLC — not John Apex, not Apex Contracting without the entity, not a parent company you’ve never heard of. Cross-check the W-9.

2. Policy dates cover the entire work period. “Active today” is not the test. “Active through the last day this sub could be on site or responsible for work” is. Policies also cancel mid-term, which is why expiration tracking matters more than a one-time review.

3. Limits match your contract, line by line. Pull the insurance exhibit and compare:

Coverage What to check
General liability Each occurrence, general aggregate, and products-completed operations aggregate. A contract requiring $1M/$2M does not accept $1M/$1M.
Auto “Any auto” versus owned/hired/non-owned only. A sub bringing company trucks on site needs owned-auto coverage.
Umbrella / excess The limit, and that it sits over GL, auto, and employers’ liability — not just one of them.
Workers’ comp Statutory coverage in every state where work happens, all three employers’ liability limits, and whether owners or officers are excluded.

4. NAIC numbers verify. Each insurer letter points to a carrier and a five-digit NAIC number. Look it up free through your state insurance department or the NAIC. A carrier name that sounds legitimate but has no valid number — or a number that belongs to a different company — is a stop-work flag.

5. ADDL INSD and SUBR WVD boxes are treated as questions, not answers. A “Y” means the broker believes an endorsement applies. It does not prove one was issued. A blank box means the policy gives your company nothing extra.

6. The description of operations survives a skeptical read. Watch for “when required by written contract” — that wording means no written contract, no coverage. And remember that a broker cannot type coverage into existence; the form itself says it does not amend the policy.

7. The certificate holder block is correct. Your full legal name and address. This proves the certificate was issued to you, which helps at audit. It does not make you an additional insured.

8. Cancellation language and signature hold up. Standard wording promises notice “in accordance with policy provisions” — generally no guaranteed number of days. If your contract requires 30 days, request the endorsement that provides it.

9. Endorsements are in hand — the step most GCs skip. The certificate is a summary. The endorsement is the contract.

Endorsement What you are verifying
CG 20 10 (additional insured, ongoing operations) You are named for work in progress, specifically or by valid blanket wording.
CG 20 37 (additional insured, completed operations) You are covered for claims arising from finished work. The most common gap in construction.
CG 24 04 (waiver of subrogation) The insurer gives up its right to sue you after paying — on GL and on workers’ comp.
Primary and non-contributory The sub’s policy responds first, with no contribution from yours.

Field rule: If you cannot point to an endorsement page with your coverage on it, assume you are not an additional insured.

Five fraud red flags

  1. Fonts, spacing, or alignment that shift around dates and limits.
  2. An expired policy presented as current, with a suspiciously clean expiration date.
  3. NAIC numbers or producer contacts that fail an independent lookup.
  4. A sub who delays or dodges requests for endorsement pages or the broker’s direct line.
  5. Coverage that is too generous for the crew — a $10M umbrella on a two-person handyman operation.

When any of these appear, do not call the number printed on the certificate. Look up the carrier or agency independently. Forged certificates often carry the forger’s phone number.

When a check fails

Three outcomes, and only three:

  • Accept — everything required is present and matches the contract. Log the date and reviewer.
  • Conditional — work starts against a written commitment with a hard deadline, logged in the file.
  • Reject — no start date until the gap closes.

FAQ

Is an ACORD 25 proof of insurance?

It is evidence that a policy existed when the certificate was issued. It grants no rights, creates no coverage, and does not amend the policy. For anything that matters, verify the endorsement pages behind it.

How old can a certificate of insurance be?

Many contracts require one dated within 30 days, but age is not the real test. What matters is whether the policy dates cover the work period — and whether the policy is still in force today.

Can a broker add additional insured status on the certificate?

No. Only the policy or an endorsement can extend coverage. The description box cannot create it, and a checked ADDL INSD box does not prove it. Ask for the endorsement form.
