Primary and Non-Contributory: What It Means and How to Verify It

November 18, 20266 min read

Two carriers, one loss, and an “other insurance” clause in each policy that decides who pays. That clause is where your loss history gets written.

A primary and non-contributory endorsement is what makes the sub’s policy pay first and pay alone. Without it, your carrier shares the loss — and your renewal prices it.

The two words, separately

Primary means the sub’s policy responds first, before yours.

Non-contributory means the sub’s insurer cannot seek a contribution from your policy.

Primary alone still leaves the door open for contribution. Non-contributory is the half that keeps your carrier out of the loss entirely.

An example. A sub’s employee damages a homeowner’s floors while installing cabinets, and the homeowner sues you as the GC. With the wording in place, the sub’s carrier defends you and pays. Without it, both carriers split the defense and the indemnity, and your loss run records a claim you did not cause.

Why the other-insurance clause decides it

Every general liability policy carries a condition governing other insurance. Standard wording makes the policy excess when other valid insurance covers the same loss, or shares it pro rata.

Absent primary and non-contributory wording, your policy is “other insurance.” The sub’s carrier invokes the condition, your carrier contributes, and the claim lands on your loss run.

A $250,000 loss split between two carriers means your carrier pays a share, your loss run records a claim, and your next renewal prices it. With the wording in place, the sub’s carrier responds first and alone.

Where the wording lives

Three places, depending on the carrier:

  1. Inside the additional insured endorsement itself.
  2. A separate primary and non-contributory endorsement.
  3. A blanket endorsement applying to additional insured status required by written contract.

The form number varies. What matters is the sentence on the page: primary, and non-contributory — or primary “without contribution.”

What it does not do

Primary and non-contributory changes the order of payment. It does not expand coverage.

  • If the sub’s policy excludes the loss, the wording does nothing.
  • If additional insured status is limited to ongoing operations, the wording does not reach completed work.
  • If the named insured is the wrong entity, the wording attaches to the wrong policy.
  • If the endorsement covers only the sub’s own negligence, your own acts are not covered by it.

Read it as a payment-order rule, not a coverage grant. That distinction is why a sub can hand you a certificate with every box checked and still leave your carrier holding half a claim.

How to verify it

  • Endorsement page in hand, not just the certificate
  • Wording says primary and non-contributory, or primary without contribution
  • It applies to additional insured status, not only the named insured
  • It reaches both ongoing and completed operations
  • Written contract requires it
  • Policy numbers and dates match the certificate
  • No conflicting excess wording elsewhere in the policy
  • The endorsement is attached to the same policy the certificate cites

There is no PNC checkbox on the ACORD 25. The description of operations cannot grant it. The endorsement is the only proof — which is what ACORD 25 and endorsement verification is for, and why it has to run again at every renewal.

Pull the policy’s “other insurance” condition and read it against the endorsement. If the condition still says excess or pro rata, the endorsement has to override it in explicit language.

The completed-operations wrinkle

Most PNC wording attaches to the additional insured endorsement, and most additional insured endorsements cover ongoing operations only. If the sub has CG 20 10 but not CG 20 37, the PNC wording may stop at the end of the job.

That is why the three requirements travel together: both additional insured forms, the waiver of subrogation, and the primary and non-contributory wording. One without the others leaves a gap at the edges.

Residential remodelers meet this on water claims more than any other loss — a supply line, a shower pan, a roof detail. The homeowner’s carrier pays, then looks for recovery, and the PNC wording decides whether your policy is in that conversation. The residential compliance requirements add the trade-specific limits.

Put it in the contract

Name it in the insurance exhibit, alongside the forms and the waiver:

“Additional Insured status on a primary and non-contributory basis for ongoing operations (ISO CG 20 10 or equivalent) and completed operations (ISO CG 20 37 or equivalent), with a waiver of subrogation in favor of [Your Company].”

FAQ

Is primary the same as non-contributory?

No. Primary sets the order of payment; non-contributory prevents the sub’s insurer from seeking contribution from yours. You want both words on the page.

Does PNC apply to completed operations?

Only if the endorsement says so. Many forms attach to the additional insured endorsement, which may itself be limited to ongoing operations. Check both documents together.

Can the certificate show PNC?

No. There is no checkbox for it. The endorsement page is the only proof, and the description of operations cannot create it.

The bottom line

Primary and non-contributory is the difference between the sub’s carrier owning the loss and your carrier sharing it. Ask for the endorsement page, read the sentence, and keep it with the certificate.
