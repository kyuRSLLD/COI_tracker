CG 20 10 vs. CG 20 37: The Completed-Operations Gap That Costs Contractors

November 18, 20266 min read

A sub’s certificate shows $1M/$2M, the ADDL INSD box is checked, and the endorsement page reads CG 20 10. Eight months after the remodel, a supply line fails inside a wall and the homeowner sues you. The sub’s carrier reads the same page and denies: that form covers work in progress, and the work was finished in March.

The gap is not exotic. It is the most common endorsement failure in construction, and it hides behind a form number most GCs never read past.

What each form actually covers

Both are ISO additional insured endorsements. They answer different claims.

Form Covers The claim it answers
CG 20 10 (2013 edition) Ongoing operations An injury or loss while the sub’s work is in progress
CG 20 37 Completed operations A defect discovered after the crew is gone
CG 20 10 (1985 edition) Both Either of the above — the broad form

Most subcontracts require both forms. Most subs carry only the first, because that is what their broker sends by default.

CG 20 37 does not create additional insured status on its own. It extends an existing grant to completed operations, which is why carriers pair it with CG 20 10 instead of issuing it alone.

The edition trap

CG 20 10 has been revised for decades, and the edition date printed after the form number changes what it covers.

  • CG 20 10 11 85 — the broad form. It covers ongoing operations and your work performed for the additional insured, which reaches completed work.
  • CG 20 10 04 13 — the current form. It limits coverage to ongoing operations and requires a written contract.

Same form number. Different coverage. A subcontract that says “CG 20 10” without an edition is ambiguous, and the carrier will read it the narrow way. Specify the edition, or specify CG 20 37 alongside it.

Blanket vs. scheduled

A blanket endorsement extends additional insured status to any entity the named insured is required by written contract to insure. Your name never appears on the form; the contract does the work.

A scheduled endorsement names specific entities, sometimes specific projects.

Scheduled forms list entity names exactly as the broker typed them. A typo, a DBA, or a project name that differs from the contract can leave your company off the endorsement while the certificate still looks complete.

The trap sits under the blanket: no written contract, no additional insured. Hire a sub by text message and the blanket never activates. That is why the insurance exhibit and the signature page matter as much as the endorsement.

What the certificate cannot tell you

The ADDL INSD checkbox says an endorsement may exist. It does not say which form, which edition, or whether completed operations is included. The description of operations can claim anything — the certificate does not amend the policy.

The only proof is the endorsement page, matched to the policy number and effective dates on the certificate.

Ask the broker for the endorsement page, not the form name. “CG 20 10” on a certificate is a label, not a description of coverage.

How to verify the package

  • Endorsement pages in hand, not just the certificate
  • Form number and edition date written into the sub file
  • Ongoing operations covered (CG 20 10 04 13 or the broad form)
  • Completed operations covered (CG 20 37 or CG 20 10 11 85)
  • Named insured matches the legal entity on your subcontract
  • Your company named, specifically or by valid blanket wording
  • Written subcontract in place that requires the coverage
  • Policy numbers and dates match the certificate
  • Expiration tracked, because the whole package lapses with the policy

Running those nine checks on every sub at every renewal is a subcontractor COI tracking problem, not a filing problem. ACORD 25 and endorsement verification is what turns it into a repeatable step instead of a scramble.

What the gap costs

Completed-operations claims arrive late and cost more. The work is closed, the crew is gone, and the damage has been sitting inside a wall. Defense alone can run five figures before anyone decides who pays.

If the sub’s carrier denies additional insured status, the claim comes to your policy. Your carrier defends it, your loss run records it, and your renewal prices it. The sub’s certificate looked perfect the whole time.

Put it in the contract

Require both forms by number and edition, and require the endorsement pages with the certificate:

“Additional Insured status on a primary and non-contributory basis for ongoing operations (ISO CG 20 10 04 13 or equivalent) and completed operations (ISO CG 20 37 04 13 or equivalent).”

FAQ

Does CG 20 37 replace CG 20 10?

No. CG 20 37 adds completed operations; it does not grant ongoing operations coverage on its own. You need both forms, or the 1985 broad form that reaches both.

Is CG 20 10 11 85 still available?

Some carriers still issue the older edition; many will not. If yours won’t, require CG 20 37 alongside the current CG 20 10 and verify both pages.

Can a broker add completed operations on the certificate?

No. Only the policy or an endorsement can extend coverage. A checked box or a line in the description of operations does not create it.

The bottom line

The certificate proves nothing about completed operations. The endorsement does. Read the form number, read the edition date, and keep both pages with the certificate.
