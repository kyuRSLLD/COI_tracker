# Consented GC/sub relationships — staged, disconnected

**Not deployed or approved for release; keep maintenance mode ON.** This PostgreSQL-only work does not remediate legacy routes or change production startup. The registration and connections routers are disconnected; only test compositions mount them. Do not use legacy authentication or expose these services without independent review.

## Evidence and authority

Successful registration with a delivered, unexpired one-time email challenge creates a **new application user, new source sub record, and new profile from scratch** in one transaction. It never finds or attaches an existing sub, company, COI, or profile by name, email, or other attributes. No COI ownership mapping is created. Possession of the email token establishes only control of that mailbox and the newly created application profile; first name, last name, phone, company and trade are self-reported. It does **not** establish legal company identity, authority to represent anyone, insurance validity, or ownership of historical COIs. Email verification is not phone verification or KYC. A signed-in account with role `gc` is likewise not an independently verified business.

The same UUID, source sub ID and user ID are stored in `auth_v2_sub_profiles` and `gc_sub_profiles`. An immutable `gc_sub_fresh_profile_evidence` row records the explicit type `fresh_self_asserted_email_control_v1`, the shared email-control reference, record time and SHA-256 challenge digest. Composite foreign keys require the full identity **and reference** to match both mappings. The digest is not a bearer token; it permits correlation while challenge records remain available, but is not itself proof of delivery or freshness and is not a substitute for tamper-resistant auditing. The old `gc_sub_profiles.verified_at` field, for these fresh rows **only**, records the email-control event; its historical name is not a business-verification claim. Existing rows are never relabeled. `auth_v2_sub_profiles` remains immutable, and evidence cannot be updated or deleted through ordinary SQL. A GC profile identity remains immutable and suspension irreversible. The new migration makes GC sub-user IDs unique; if existing data violates this or contains cross-table identity conflicts, it aborts rather than silently changing data.

Cross-table triggers serialize competing identity inserts and reject a different UUID/sub/user mapping sharing any identity component. These constraints protect identity consistency, not the truth of the evidence: a privileged writer can fabricate profiles, references, challenge digests or evidence, disable triggers, or change schema. Only the trusted signup transaction may create fresh evidence. Do not give untrusted users, legacy application roles, or generic service accounts INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE on these profile/evidence tables, control over triggers/DDL, or direct arbitrary SQL. Use a narrowly scoped separate provisioning principal, dedicated migration principal, fixed safe search path, and least privileges on the tables, functions, sequences and role-change trigger writes. Isolate all older writers and independently audit DB permissions, source-record creation and trigger ownership. External append-only audit is still needed. No RLS or independently trustworthy proof/attestation is implemented.

Any previous `gc_sub_profiles` or `auth_v2_sub_profiles` rows without the explicit fresh evidence remain **unknown/unclassified**, even if they were previously documented as independently verified, have a prefix/reference, or happen to match. The updated service refuses to issue/redeem QR or invite, preview/accept or revoke for those profiles, and excludes them from reads, even if an old grant exists. Their existing rows, capabilities, and grants are not rewritten or deleted. No existing record is promoted. A separate reviewed, auditable process is required to classify or transition historical records and clean up their dormant permissions. Do not merely add a fresh evidence row to an old profile.

## Consent and data scope

`CONSENT_V2` (`share-self-reported-profile-only-v2`) is a **new** exact consent version. The exact strings `QR_CONSENT_V2_TEXT` and `INVITE_CONSENT_V2_TEXT` are served by the router; render the appropriate string in full and require a separate affirmative action. They disclose the profile identifiers and email-control evidence type, current **and future** self-reported company name and trade, the unverified recipient/business and insurance/authority limits, and that neither COIs nor documents are shared. This is an intentional product/security scope change. The previous v1 constants describe the former contract for historical reference only and are no longer accepted for creation or redemption; old grants are not valid v2 consent. No field should be presented as a verified company or verified insurance.

A sub can discover only their own eligible profile via `getOwnProfile` / `GET /profile`. After affirmative v2 consent, `issueSubQr` returns a five-minute random capability for local QR encoding. Whichever authenticated, live-role GC account scans it can redeem once, atomically creating/updating a grant and grant event. This QR is **not** targeted to a specific GC; protect it. For a named invite, independently authorize the GC's recipient choice, securely deliver the seven-day token, have the sub preview the inviter and exact invitation text, then affirm v2. Neither recipient-policy approval nor invite creation is consent. The router cannot prove a human saw the disclosure; usable, accessible UI and durable consent-presentation evidence remain release blockers.

`listGrantedSubs` returns only sub record ID, self-reported company name/trade, profile ID, and explicit evidence type for active fresh v2 grants. The sub account must still be enabled and email-verified, and the user must still hold the `sub` role. An old v1 grant, unknown profile, suspended profile or disabled/downgraded sub does not qualify. `listGrantedCois` deliberately returns an empty list: even an independently inserted `gc_sub_coi_ownership` row does not expand the new consent. There is no file access. A future COI/document workflow needs separate authorization, truthful scope-specific consent, ownership evidence, review and migration. Never auto-map historical COIs or infer ownership from the GC owner map. A separate private-file service still uses explicit COI ownership mappings; introducing fresh profile rows does not authorize creating such mappings, and that separate service and all other consumers require independent cross-service review before deployment.

The exact sub owner may revoke a particular GC grant; this also invalidates all unspent profile QRs and that GC's pending invitations. Other grants remain. New affirmative consent can create a new grant. Role-change triggers invalidate outstanding relevant capabilities and revoke grants with `revoked_role` events. A disabled/re-enabled account loses sessions, but existing grants are not automatically revoked merely by disablement; after re-enablement and login they may resume if both sides are otherwise eligible. Operators must decide whether stronger offboarding is needed. Existing unclassified profiles cannot use this service to revoke their dormant historical grants; an authorized administrative repair is needed.

Tokens are 32 random bytes; only SHA-256 digests are stored. Do not put credentials or capabilities in URLs, logs, analytics, referrers, screenshots, browser history or untrusted caches. Use TLS and no-store handling. Grant events record action, profile, actors, version and time but are not tamper-proof and do not prove business identity.

## Authentication, races and release gates

Each service operation independently verifies a strict RS256 JWT, issuer, single audience, `sub`, `jti`, `iat`, expiration and maximum lifetime, then locks/checks the live actor role, enabled email-verified account and unrevoked/unexpired hashed session. Other actors' roles are checked; GC-side profile writes lock the sub's enabled account, and invite acceptance checks the inviter's enabled account. Preview/list reads use PostgreSQL statement snapshots and check the relevant enabled account and roles. Data already returned cannot be recalled, and a read started before revocation or disablement may return an earlier snapshot. Logout/disable/role changes acquiring relevant locks first deny an operation; an operation that locks first may finish before the change commits. JWT/DB expiry is rechecked before commit, but may pass just after the last check. Policy authorization for invitations is a separate non-atomic callback. The disclosure endpoint depends on trusted authentication middleware; the service's checks do not replace ingress, a trusted verifier or abuse limits. Errors must fail closed, and whole-transaction retry policy must consider deadlocks and serialization failures.

Before any independently authorized deployment, review all legacy and indirect routes, owner/file services and their permissions/races, consent UI and accessible localized copy, recipient policy and secure delivery, signup abuse prevention, account recovery/disable/delete, audit retention, actual schema and role privileges, TLS/CSRF, operational observability and incident response. Existing routes remain unsafe; no live DB, real provider or deployment was exercised.

## Forward-only migration

Old migration files and their checksums are unchanged. On a dedicated idle PostgreSQL 16 session in a trusted schema, explicitly verify/apply `migrateGcSubConsentV1`, `migrateAuthV2V1` and `migrateRegistrationV2V1`, then `migrateFreshSubProfileEvidenceV1` (`005_fresh_sub_profile_evidence.ts`). The new independent `fresh_sub_profile_schema_migrations` ledger checks its own checksum and requires exactly version 1 of each prior ledger; **it checks prior version presence, not prior checksums or actual schema drift**. Explicitly invoke the prior migration functions to verify their checksums and separately inspect the real schema. New DDL and ledger insertion are transactional; no startup runner or destructive down migration is supplied. Adding unique indexes/constraints may fail on historical duplicates, conflicting mappings or schema drift, and can lock tables. Stop all writers and retain maintenance mode; backup, verify restore, inspect historical conflicts without automatic repair, rehearse on a sanitized clone, assess migration privileges and trigger effects, verify all constraints/counts afterward, and independently review before a separately authorized operation. No backfill occurs. Forward repair or verified restore is required after a committed migration.

## Local test

From an isolated worktree with dependencies, Node 22 and local PostgreSQL 16 binaries, run `scripts/test-fresh-sub-registration.sh`. It creates and destroys a new loopback-only cluster and five synthetic databases, strips inherited PG settings and uses only synthetic credentials and mock delivery. It tests full names/US phone, email possession and transaction rollback, old-data non-claim, explicit provenance and immutable same-identity constraints, concurrent conflicting mappings, stale/unknown consent, QR and invitation lifecycle, no COI sharing even with mappings, cross-tenant denial and session/role/revocation races, including GC-owner suites. Snapshot tests retain v2 consent and do not downgrade it. The test fixture's evidence inserts are synthetic only, not a real migration method. URL/name guardrails do not by themselves prove isolation. Tests are not a production-schema rehearsal or security audit.
